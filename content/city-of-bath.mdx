# The Roman baths of Bath

![The Great Bath at the Roman Baths in Bath, England](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3d/Roman_Baths_in_Bath_Spa%2C_England_-_July_2006.jpg/640px-Roman_Baths_in_Bath_Spa%2C_England_-_July_2006.jpg)

The city of Bath, in the south-west of England, is home to the only <Col n={1}>naturally hot
springs</Col> in Britain. The Romans, who <Vocab word="valued" vi="coi trọng; đánh giá cao" />
the <Col n={2}>healing properties</Col> of the water, built a grand <Col n={3}>bathing and temple
<Vocab word="complex" vi="quần thể; tổ hợp" /></Col> around the spring in the 1st century AD,

At the heart of the site lies the Great Bath, a <Col n={4}>lead-lined pool</Col> fed by water
that still rises at a temperature of around 46 °C. Surrounding it were changing rooms, saunas
and <Col n={5}>cold plunge pools</Col>, reflecting the <Vocab word="elaborate" vi="cầu kỳ; công phu" />
<Col n={6}>bathing rituals</Col> of Roman society. Adjoining the baths stood a temple
<Vocab word="dedicated" vi="dâng hiến; thờ phụng" /> to the goddess Sulis Minerva, where
worshippers left offerings and curses inscribed on sheets of lead.

## Decline and rediscovery

After the Romans withdrew from Britain in the early 5th century, the baths <Col n={7}>fell into
disrepair</Col> and were gradually <Vocab word="buried" vi="bị chôn vùi" /> beneath silt and
later medieval buildings. It was not until the late 19th century that the site was extensively
<Vocab word="excavated" vi="đào bới; khai quật" />, revealing the remarkably well-preserved
structures visible today. The Roman Baths are now among the most visited <Col n={8}>heritage
sites</Col> in Britain, drawing more than a million visitors each year.
